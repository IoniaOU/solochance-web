Solo pools aren't one-size-fits-all — they differ in payout scheme (winner-takes-all, proportional-since-last-block, or top-N share splitting), fees, latency, and minimum withdrawal. The pools below are ones we've used or vetted directly, grouped by the kind of experience they offer.

Pools built and hosted by SoloChance.org with unique payment schemes that break from the traditional "whole block to one winner" model — designed to give small-hashrate miners more frequent, meaningful outcomes instead of near-zero odds.

The Classic Experience

Established, trusted pools with a proven track record — from strict solo (finder keeps the block) to proportional-since-last-block payouts, without the newer leaderboard-style schemes above. For miners who want reliable, time-tested options.

How to Choose a Solo Pool

  • Fee — ranges from 0% (self-hosted options like publicpool.io) to ~1.5% (atlaspool.io's global Anycast infrastructure). Lower isn't automatically better if it costs you uptime or latency.
  • Payout scheme — strict solo (finder takes all, e.g. ckpool.org), proportional-since-last-block (parasite.space, bch.ee), or share-leaderboard hybrids (solo100.org). Pick based on whether you want pure lottery odds or smoothed-out volatility.
  • Latency — matters more than people expect for small miners; a pool with regional/Anycast routing (atlaspool.io) reduces rejected shares versus a single distant server.
  • Self-hosting — if you want full sovereignty over your node and payouts, publicpool.io is open-source and self-hostable rather than only available as a hosted service.
